# Genres

Natural prose in one genre is wrong prose in another. Classify the piece before editing it.
If the genre is ambiguous, infer it from the draft and the assignment; ask only when the
answer would change the rewrite substantially.

The single most common way to ruin a draft while humanising it is to make it casual. A
human lab report is not chatty. It is a lab report written by a person who knew what they
were measuring.

## Register at a glance

| Genre | First person | Contractions | Fragments | Hedging | Passive | Sensory detail | Reader address |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Academic argument | discipline-dependent | no | no | matched to evidence | sparing | no | no |
| Literary analysis | often yes | no | rare | yes, on readings | rare | quoted text only | no |
| History, social science | rarely | no | no | yes, on causation | some | no | no |
| STEM report | no | no | no | disciplined | yes, standard | no | no |
| Technical documentation | second person | yes | short imperatives | minimal | avoid | no | yes |
| Business memo | yes | yes | occasional | on forecasts only | avoid | no | yes |
| Journalism | rarely | some | yes | attributed | rare | yes | no |
| Blog, opinion | yes | yes | yes | yes | rare | some | yes |
| Personal essay | yes | yes | yes | yes | rare | yes, as evidence | sometimes |
| Application essay | yes | yes | some | some | no | yes, specific | no |
| Fiction | as chosen | yes | yes | in voice | rare | selective | permitted |

## Academic argument

Priority order: assignment compliance, factual and citation integrity, a clear thesis,
evidence-specific reasoning, real qualification, natural formal prose, sentence variety.

**Opening.** Avoid the funnel: since the beginning of time, broad importance, three
reasons, thesis. Better openings are a precise problem, a disagreement in the literature, a
definition the argument needs, a specific case, or the thesis itself. Do not force a hook if
the discipline does not want one.

**Thesis.** Make a claim, not an announcement. Specify the scope. Do not claim more
certainty than the evidence supports. `This essay will discuss` only if the assignment asks
for roadmap language.

**Body paragraphs.** Vary the move. A paragraph can run claim → evidence → analysis, or
evidence → puzzle → interpretation, or source A → source B → the distinction, or case →
implication, or counterexample → narrowed thesis, or definition → application. One
controlling purpose per paragraph, not one template for all of them.

**Sources.** Prefer `Smith finds X under condition Y, which matters here because...` over
`According to Smith, X. This highlights the importance of Y.` Show the relationship between
the evidence and the argument. Do not add a citation because a sentence sounds academic.

**Counterargument.** Only if real. It should change the thesis, its scope, or its
confidence. Attributable to a source, to a position held in the field, or to a genuine
logical alternative.

**Conclusion.** Not a restatement of the introduction. Synthesise two findings, state the
limit of the claim, show a practical implication, name the unresolved question, or return to
the opening case now read differently. No vague optimism.

## Literary analysis

Preserve close reading. A named textual detail persuades; a generic thematic statement does
not.

Avoid plot summary standing in for analysis, claiming an image "symbolises" something
without textual support, abstract claims that would fit any novel, and "this shows" after
every quotation.

Prefer exact word choice, syntax, narrative perspective, a recurring contrast, structural
placement, tension between two passages, and competing readings where both are defensible.

## History and social science

Keep these separate: chronology, causation, correlation, interpretation, primary evidence,
secondary scholarship.

Do not smooth multiple causes into one elegant narrative when the sources disagree. Use
dates and named actors specifically when the material provides them. Where historians
disagree, say so rather than resolving it for them.

## STEM report

Do not humanise by making it chatty. That is the main risk in this genre.

Keep: technical terms, standard passive constructions, explicit methods, chronological
procedure, disciplined uncertainty, conventional section headings.

Improve: vague claims, unnecessary adjectives, stock transitions, inflated significance,
unsupported causal language, conclusions that overreach the data.

Never alter reported results to improve flow.

## Technical documentation

Optimise for clarity, not personality. Second person and imperatives are correct here.

Use simple verbs, explicit actors, stable terminology (do not vary the word for the same
thing), concrete examples drawn from the actual system, short headings, direct
instructions.

Do not add jokes, anecdotes, hedging, or ambiguity. Do not describe what the previous
version did.

## Business memo

Lead with the recommendation. Then the reasoning, then the evidence, then what you need
from the reader.

Contractions and first person are normal. Hedge only on forecasts, and give the range
rather than the hedge. A memo that ends with "stakeholders must work together" has not made
a recommendation.

## Journalism and feature writing

Attribution is the spine. Every claim traces to a named person or document. Sensory detail
is reporting, not decoration, and it belongs where the reporter was present.

Avoid the stock nut graf that explains the significance of the story before the story has
happened.

## Blog and opinion

The writer's actual opinions, their uncertainty, their mixed feelings, their humour, and
their asides are the point. Preserve them. If the writer would react to something, a
reaction is allowed; an invented fact is not.

Avoid the engagement template: hook question, three-part promise, listicle body,
inspirational close.

## Personal essay and application essay

Voice and selection matter more than polish.

Preserve: specific memories the writer supplied, mixed feelings, uncertainty, idiosyncratic
observation, real humour, unusual but meaningful sentence patterns.

Never invent childhood details, family stories, adversity, sensory memories, dialogue, or
achievements. This is the genre where fabrication does the most damage, because the whole
form rests on the claim that these things happened.

Avoid turning every event into a lesson. An essay can end with a changed view without
spelling out a universal moral. The instinct to close with the takeaway is the single
strongest machine tendency measured in narrative writing.

## Fiction

Invented detail is the task, so the fabrication rule does not apply to the content. Every
structural check in `references/structure.md` applies most directly here.

Audit for: explicit moral commentary in the closing paragraphs, a single-track plot,
resolution through the protagonist understanding something, emotion rendered as bodily
sensation every time, decorative sensory density, characters introduced by physical
description, strictly linear disclosure, vague allusion where a named reference would do,
and moral certainty that flattens a character's choice.

Do not mechanically invert these. The goal is a wider range of plausible authorial choices,
not a rule that every story must be fragmented, ambiguous, and referential.
