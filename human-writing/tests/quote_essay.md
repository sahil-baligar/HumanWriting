Okafor opens with an image of appetite. The harbour is "a grey mouth that swallowed the boats one by one" (Okafor, 1), and the town's silence about it is a kind of respect, since "the old men said it listened" (Okafor, 1).

The second chapter turns that appetite into bookkeeping. Mara's father "stopped counting the ones who did not come back" (Okafor, 2), because in his view "the sea kept its own ledger" (Okafor, 2).
